What does a one-megawatt battery earn in Danish balancing?

When Denmark's grid runs short, Energinet activates reserves and pays every activated megawatt the marginal price; when it runs long, an activated megawatt takes energy at a price below the day-ahead price, sometimes below zero. Those prices are public for every hour since 2019. This page puts a one-megawatt, two-hour battery in that market as a price taker, fixes its rules on the even weeks of 2021 to 2023, and replays fourteen months it never saw. Every number is recomputed in your browser from the same per-hour prices the build script used.

Before the battery · five things to know

Denmark has two price zones, DK1 (Jutland and Funen) and DK2 (Zealand). Each hour has a day-ahead price set in an auction the day before. Inside the hour, Energinet keeps the grid balanced by activating reserves: up-regulation when the system is short (more production, less consumption), down-regulation when it is long.

An activated megawatt of up-regulation is paid the up price, the price of the last bid Energinet needed; every activated bid gets that price. An activated megawatt of down-regulation consumes energy and pays the down price, which sits below the day-ahead price and can be negative, in which case the megawatt is paid to consume. When a direction was not activated, Energinet records its price as equal to the day-ahead price.

The battery here is 1 MW with 2.2 MWh of usable store, 90 percent round-trip efficiency, and a cycle cost of 30 euros per megawatt-hour delivered. It bids whole megawatts for whole hours, the Danish minimum, and lives only in this market: it charges only when activated for down-regulation and discharges only when activated for up-regulation.

A price taker: a bid at or below the marginal price counts as activated at that price. That is right for one megawatt except at the margin, where the last bid may be partly activated; the page says so and lets you require a safety margin in act three. An hour counts as activated only when mFRR energy actually ran; hours where the price moved on the automatic reserve alone do not count. Activations can also end inside the hour; act three prices that too. The battery must be prequalified with Energinet; that is the asset owner's status, not a modelling choice.

Every rule was fixed on the even weeks of November 2021 to December 2023 and then frozen. January 2024 to 4 March 2025, where Energinet's hourly series ends, was replayed once and is called the separate period.

Asset card, failures, limits, terms

What was assumed, what went wrong, and what this cannot say

Asset card

The battery lives only in the activation market on this page. A real operator stacks the day-ahead market, capacity payments for aFRR and mFRR reserves, and activations; the activation income here is one layer of that stack, not the whole.

Honest failures
dataEnerginet's hourly regulating power series (RegulatingBalancePowerdata) to 4 March 2025: up and down regulation prices, activated mFRR volumes, the imbalance price; the day-ahead price carried by the tavle platform. The 15-minute successor that began in March 2025 uses a new price design and is not loaded. splitEven ISO weeks of November 2021 to December 2023 chose the margins. The separate period was recomputed twice more: when prices were rounded for the browser replay, and when an audit tightened the activation flag to require mFRR energy in the hour, which re-chose the margins and moved the headline. Limits

Price taker: the marginal bid may be partly activated and bids have location limits; act three's safety margin shows the cost of being conservative about that, and its partial-hour chip scales delivery by the system's activated volume over 60 megawatt-hours, a crude proxy, capped at one. Whole-hour, whole-megawatt bids; a real battery also trades the day-ahead market and sells reserve capacity, none of which is here. The cycle cost and the efficiency are stated assumptions; the annual cost of a two-hour battery in the verdict is an external figure, not computed here. Energinet's hourly series ends 4 March 2025 and the market moved to quarter-hours and a new price design; the separate period stops there. Two zones, one country, one author who chose the questions.

Seven terms

up-regulation reserves activated when the system is short; an activated megawatt is paid the up price
down-regulation reserves activated when the system is long; an activated megawatt consumes and pays the down price, which can be negative
marginal price the price of the last bid Energinet needed in the hour; every activated bid in that direction gets it
price taker a bid small enough not to move the price; at or below the marginal price it counts as activated
margin what the battery adds to the day-ahead price to offer a discharge, or subtracts to offer a charge
even weeks the half of the training years on which every choice was made
separate period January 2024 to 4 March 2025, replayed once after every choice was frozen
